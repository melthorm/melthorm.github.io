If one never makes a serious post, how should he know whether he has the ability to? Maybe he has even lost that ability. Or maybe it never existed.

I have, upon other things, exercised a truly exorbitant level of self-deprecation within previous blog posts. At a certain point though, self-deprecation becomes not a mark of humility nor anything even funny but instead a great, large signpost of a great, large lack of skill. It is unfortunate that I had to resort to `that` in order to make the previous sentence not sound like AI. It is my opinion that 5 years ago, that sentence -- in the original form that I had imagined it in, the form now harassed and mutated by AI -- would've been, perhaps not more interesting to read, but certainly an objectively better sentence. Holy yap. Also what vitriol from someone wholly unbecoming to give it. 

I suppose some things will not change.

***

The purpose of today's post is to discuss pseudo-intellectualism. That word which ended the previous sentence, for this post's context, takes the position of vibes over substance. I, and every human writer (perhaps even one could extend this position to the quote on quote AI writer, however stupid I may believe them to be), is guilty of that sin. We over-complicate, we place our own little curtsies, we strangely phrase for emphasis, we murder convention, we are contentious over the color purple, we take great care in the aesthetic, but our end goal -- the end goal of every form of communication -- should be to make the recipient feel something. 


A great tragedy of this world is that great ideas must be wrapped up in increasingly convoluted aesthetics. Of course, this is not true in everything.

## This post is late or tardy or a truant (what?)
![stsdfs](/assets/postImages/Pseudo-intelectttuallism/3.png "I dunno bro")
`I have been very busy`

So, this post has the supposed date of release listed as September 9, 2026. It is currently September 20, 2026, at 12:34 PM. Why is this post late?

It is for a frankly simple reason. I have said, many times before on this blog, that I waste time indiscriminately. It is hard to detract from this perspective. I believe that so long as there is a single minute of a day spent on something 'non-productive', that day will contain wasted time. If I even speak to somebody without doing something 'productive' concurrently, time will have been wasted. In other words, it is impossible to have a day that does not contain wasted time. Is this a healthy perspective? It is not by any stretch. However, I think this perspective is a much 'better' one than any other. If you ask me to explain or argue that point, I would simply flounder about and put out something nonsensical, so this perspective is without doubt illogical. We humans are not perfect machines though. We are illogical. We are instinctive. And that is that.

This post is late due to me not finding the time to complete it. Last weekend, I had to work on a stackless coroutines programming assignment. That was painful. Objectively speaking, the assignment did not nearly take as much time as I expected, but debugging is so much more taxing than any other task. Debugging makes me hate programming. We are making a kernel though! That is cool. Ahmed Gheith is the goat! I also had a micro test last week which convinced me moreover that CS is the most difficult and time-intensive major. Also, that class requires you to buy $110 in some BS learning materials. It is an actual scam. I am unsure if the professor gets a kickback, but it pisses me off either way. I am already paying 10k for tuition, so why should I have to pay money within a fucking class as well. I am angry. And yet, my language has without doubt regressed.

My typical schedule on the weekends is to complete the microbiology mCgRaW hIlL coNnECt and the associated quiz, watch the 2 hours of biochemistry videos my professor assigns and do the associated quiz, read over the lecture notes for abstract algebra and complete the PSet, read over the slides in Game Theory and complete the associated PSet, and complete whatever OS prog was assigned. I lowkey am skipping two of my classes. Actually, I am kind of skipping 4. The reasoning makes sense though! If going to a lecture is actively detrimental to my learning progress, why should I go to lecture? The only other reason is for a grade (ie. attendence or quiz). For the four classes I am typically skipping, I think I learn faster and more concretely by reading over lecture notes and doing problems myself. Lectures are abhorrently slow. There is an argument that people have made which goes the point of college is to talk to professors. I wholly disagree. The point of college is what you make of it. You get out what you put in. For me, I do not want to talk with some of my professors. That sounds mean and pretentious. My point is, that some professors are more interesting than others so thus there will be an order relation (order relation mentioned?!). That 'purpose of college' argument is much worse than the other one which says the point of college is to learn. I don't believe that argument is a proper rebuttal to my logic of skipping. Nor do I believe that argument is even entirely correct. 

The point of college is two-fold: to learn and to earn a degree. If college did not provide a degree that society valued, nobody (or very few) would attend college -- especially considering the price tag that is attached to attending an university. Frankly, if you are solely maximizing learning, it is my belief that you will learn much more by being an autodidact. Simply search up textbooks in whatever subject you want to learn and read them. If you are stuck, consult with an LLM. That is the way to maximize learning. Now, for most people, this sort of life is impossible (for me, it is certainly impossible). That is why I actually give some level of credence to the idea that one goes to college to learn (ie. the counterargument of being an autodidact fails on some level). Community is very beneficial.
